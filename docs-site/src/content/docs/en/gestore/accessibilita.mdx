The booking page is where your customers meet the beach online: it has to work for people who use only the
keyboard, a screen reader, zoom or an old phone. Arenile targets **WCAG 2.1 level AA** (the international
guidelines referenced by the European standard EN 301 549) on the public page, on the widget embedded in your
website and on the customer's "Manage your booking" area; the main flows of the panel are checked against the
same criteria.

This page says what is supported, how we verify it, what is **not** perfect yet and how to write your beach's
accessibility statement.

## Why it matters to you: the European Accessibility Act

Since **28 June 2025** the European Accessibility Act (Directive (EU) 2019/882, transposed in Italy by Legislative
Decree 82/2022) requires e-commerce services for consumers, online bookings included, to be accessible, and the
provider to explain clearly how they are. **Microenterprises** providing services (fewer than 10 employees and an
annual turnover or balance sheet of up to 2 million euro) are exempt from the obligations, but an accessible page
is still a page more customers can use.

<Aside type="caution">
  Arenile gives you a tool designed and tested to be accessible, but the service remains the beach's
  responsibility: it also depends on what you publish (texts, logo, colours, the website hosting the widget). Ask
  your advisor whether and how the law applies to you.
</Aside>

## What is supported

### Keyboard

- The whole booking can be completed **without a mouse**: dates, spot, package and extras, customer details, terms
  and confirmation. An automated test repeats it with the keyboard only on every code change.
- **Calendar:** Enter opens it, the arrows move by one day, Page Down and Page Up by one month; Enter twice on the
  same day picks a single day. Escape closes it and focus returns to the dates field.
- **Map:** the map is a single tab stop; the arrows move from spot to spot, Home and End go to the first and last,
  Enter or Space choose. The **Show as a list** button offers the same choice as a list of buttons.
- Packages and time slots are option groups: Tab enters the group, the arrows change the choice.
- **Focus is always visible** (a ring around the active element) and, on every step change, it moves to the title
  of the new step; after the confirmation it goes to the title of the outcome.
- Confirmation dialogs (for example "Cancel the booking") keep the focus while open, Escape closes them and focus
  returns to the button that opened them.

### Screen readers

- The page has landmarks (header, main content, footer), headings in order and a **language** attribute that
  follows the chosen language (Italian, English, German or French).
- Every spot on the map has a full name, for example "Spot A1: Free, from €30.00"; unavailable spots are announced
  as such and cannot be chosen.
- The **list of spots**, grouped by row, is the non-visual alternative to the map: each button says spot, status
  and the "from" price when shown.
- Calendar days say whether they are full, closed, not bookable online or have the last spots.
- Updates are announced without moving the cursor: the recalculated price, the time left to complete the booking
  (announced at intervals, not every second), the payment outcome when it goes from "pending" to "confirmed".
- Form errors are linked to their field and announced; on submit the cursor goes to the first field to fix. The
  same holds for errors returned by the server in the panel.
- Images and icons have a text alternative when they carry information (the QR code, for example) and are ignored
  when they are only decorative.

### Contrast and colour

- Interface colours meet **4.5:1** contrast for text and **3:1** for field borders and the focus ring, in **light
  and dark** theme; an automated test checks every colour pair.
- When you choose your beach's main colour, Arenile works out the text colour on top of it (white or dark) so it
  stays readable, whatever colour you pick.
- In the calendar and in the list of spots colour is never the only cue: full or closed days are **struck
  through** as well as dimmed, days with the last spots have a **dot** and the list writes each spot's status out.
  For the map see the [known limits](#known-limits).

### Motion, zoom and small screens

- If the device asks for **reduced motion**, animations, transitions and scrolling become instant.
- The page reads without horizontal scrolling down to **320 px** wide, that is with the browser zoomed to 200 % and
  beyond (checked by an automated test at every step).
- On the public page buttons and touch areas are at least **44 px** tall; small spots on the map zoom in on the
  first tap so the customer does not pick the wrong one.

### Languages

The public page, the emails and the panel are in **Italian, English, German and French**. The texts you write
(introduction, terms, emails) are shown in the customer's language only if you translated them: without a
translation the customer sees your default language.

## How we verify it

- **axe-core** (the most widely used automated checker) runs on every code change against every booking step, the
  calendar, the map and the list, the outcome, the customer area with its actions open and the cancel dialog, the
  error pages and the main panel pages (Today, Map, Bookings, Cash desk, Quick sale, Tickets, Gate, public page,
  payments and email settings). Serious or critical violations of WCAG 2.1 A and AA criteria block the release.
- A **keyboard-only test** completes a booking from start to confirmation and checks at every step that focus is
  visible and in the right place.
- Dedicated tests check colour contrast in both themes, the 320 px width, the page language and reduced motion.

## Known limits

Plainly, what is not yet as we would like:

- **The map is a drawing.** It works with a screen reader, but the list of spots is easier; on the map the
  difference between free and unavailable spots is mostly given by tone (unavailable spots are dimmed). The full
  status is in each spot's name and in the list.
- **Names of the list buttons.** The spoken name of the list buttons joins spot and status with a dash the
  visible text lacks ("A1 – Free" against "A1 Free"): voice control users saying the visible text may not be
  recognised. It is the only automated check turned off, on that view, until it is fixed.
- **Long lists.** In the list every free spot is a tab stop: on a beach with many spots it takes several Tab
  presses to reach "Next" (Shift+Tab goes back).
- **Online payment.** The payment page is Stripe's, not Arenile's: its accessibility depends on Stripe.
- **Your content.** You write the logo, introduction, terms and emails: use clear sentences, do not put important
  information only in an image and check the contrast of your logo on its background.
- **The website hosting the widget.** Arenile is accessible inside its frame; the rest of your website depends on
  whoever built it.
- **Map editor.** Drawing the beach in the panel needs a mouse or touch; spot properties (name, row, illustration)
  can also be edited with the keyboard in the side panel.
- **No external audit yet.** Checks are automated and manual on the code; there has not yet been an audit with
  people who use assistive technology. Reports are welcome (see below).

## Accessibility statement template

Publish the statement on the beach's website (for example on an "Accessibility" page linked from the footer or near
the widget) and update it when something changes. Copy the template, replace the parts in square brackets and
remove what does not apply.

<Steps>

1. Fill in the beach's name, the address of the booking page and the date.
2. List any content of yours that is not accessible (for example a scanned PDF price list).
3. Give a contact customers can report problems to and the time within which you answer.

</Steps>

```text
Accessibility statement – [Beach name]

[Company name] is committed to making its online booking service accessible, available at
[https://…/b/beach-name] and on the website [https://www.beachname.com].

Conformance
The booking service uses Arenile, designed to meet WCAG 2.1 level AA (standard EN 301 549).
The service is [partially conformant]: the content that is not accessible is listed below.

How to use it
- The whole booking can be completed with the keyboard only.
- It works with screen readers; as an alternative to the map of spots there is a list,
  opened with the "Show as a list" button.
- Text meets minimum contrast, in the calendar and in the list of spots colour is not the
  only way to show status, the page adapts to zoom up to 200 % and follows the device's "reduce motion"
  preference.
- It is available in Italian, English, German and French.

Content that is not accessible
- On the map of spots the difference between free and unavailable spots is shown mainly by
  colour; the list of spots writes the status out.
- Online payment happens on Stripe's page, whose accessibility depends on Stripe.
- [Other content of the beach that is not accessible, for example: "The PDF price list is
  not accessible; prices are shown during the booking anyway."]

Alternatives
If you cannot book online you can book by phone at [number], by email at [email address] or
at the beach's cash desk, on the same terms.

Feedback
To report an accessibility problem or ask for information in an accessible format, write to
[email address]. We answer within [10] working days.
[If the service falls under the European Accessibility Act, also name the authority the
customer can turn to if the answer is not satisfactory, as advised by your consultant.]

Date of the statement: [dd/mm/yyyy]. Last updated: [dd/mm/yyyy].
```

## Reporting a problem in Arenile

If you find an accessibility problem in the booking page or the panel, open an issue in the
[repository](https://github.com/coste360/arenile/issues) with the page, the browser, the assistive technology you
used and what you expected. Accessibility reports have the same priority as bugs.
